02 / EMBEDDED SYSTEMS

Study Space Occupancy Detector

A low-cost, bidirectional occupancy tracker built around a real problem: finding a seat during finals.

Team prototypeRead project report ↗Download PDF ↓View source on GitHub ↗

The challenge

Students can spend valuable study time searching for open seats during finals week. Our team designed StudyTrack to estimate room occupancy automatically, with low cost and no check-in step for people passing through a doorway.

What we built

Two ultrasonic sensors feed distance readings to a micro:bit. The order of detection indicates entry or exit, allowing the system to update the occupancy count. A second micro:bit receives information over radio and acts as the prototype display.

My contribution

I worked with David Tran and Jack Farley on the occupancy-detector prototype. I authored the report’s results and discussion, conclusions, and recommendations, analyzing prototype performance, counting limitations, and potential improvements.

Results and limitations

The team report records a unit cost within the $15 target and successful controlled entry/exit trials. These trials do not establish real-world accuracy. Closely spaced people, groups, unusual walking speeds, and non-human objects can produce miscounts because the logic includes a pause between detections.

Next iteration

Evaluate alternatives to the fixed detection pause, test the system in a busy doorway, and explore thermal sensing to distinguish people from objects. A student-facing app or website and support for multiple entrances remain future work.

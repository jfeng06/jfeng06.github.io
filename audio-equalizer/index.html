03 / SIGNAL PROCESSING

Three-Band Audio Equalizer

Splitting, shaping, and recombining audio with three filter bands and independent level controls.

Core stages validatedRead project report ↗Download PDF ↓

The challenge

Design an analog circuit that adjusts the relative loudness of bass, midrange, and treble. The signal chain combines filtering, isolated level adjustment, recombination, and a final speaker-driving power stage.

What I built

I split the input into low-pass, band-pass, and high-pass paths targeting crossover frequencies of 320 Hz and 3.2 kHz. Potentiometers provide independent band-level adjustment. Op-amp buffers reduce loading, and an inverting summing amplifier recombines the three paths.

Measured performance

Frequency-response measurements placed the filter cutoffs within the specified ±10% tolerance. With all controls at maximum, the recombination output measured 100 mVrms at 100 Hz, 101 mVrms at 1 kHz, and 100 mVrms at 10 kHz. The report records 3.99 mVrms of ripple.

An unresolved power-stage issue

The filtering and recombination stages met their measured requirements, but the final LM386 amplifier did not meet the 400 mW target into an 8 Ω load. Testing revealed low AC output and an unexplained DC offset. The cause was not isolated; the power stage remains an unresolved part of the build.

What I learned

Validating each stage independently made it possible to separate successful filtering from a downstream amplification problem. The project developed my practical understanding of loading, buffering, gain, and frequency-response measurements.
